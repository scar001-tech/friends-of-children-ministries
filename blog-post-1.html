10 Ways to Keep Kids Engaged During Sunday School

May 15, 2026 8 min read Sarah Johnson

As Sunday School teachers, we've all experienced those moments when children's attention starts to wander. Whether it's fidgeting, side conversations, or glazed-over looks, maintaining engagement can be one of our biggest challenges. But it doesn't have to be this way.

After years of teaching and observing successful children's ministry programs, I've compiled these ten proven strategies that will help you keep kids engaged, excited, and eager to learn about God's Word.

1. Start with High Energy

The first five minutes set the tone for your entire lesson. Begin with an energetic song, a fun game, or an intriguing question that captures their curiosity. Children respond to enthusiasm, so bring your energy and excitement about the lesson.

2. Use Interactive Storytelling

Don't just read the Bible story—bring it to life! Use different voices for characters, incorporate sound effects, and invite children to act out parts of the story. When kids are participants rather than passive listeners, they stay engaged and remember the lesson better.

3. Incorporate Movement

Children have natural energy that needs an outlet. Build movement into your lesson through:

  • Action songs with hand motions
  • Bible verse relay races
  • Freeze dance with biblical themes
  • Acting out Bible stories
  • Scavenger hunts for lesson-related items

4. Ask Engaging Questions

Replace yes/no questions with open-ended ones that require thinking. Instead of "Did David trust God?" ask "How do you think David felt when he faced Goliath?" This encourages deeper reflection and keeps minds active.

5. Use Visual Aids

Children are visual learners. Enhance your lessons with:

  • Colorful posters and illustrations
  • Props and costumes
  • Videos and animations
  • Flannel boards or magnetic boards
  • Real objects that relate to the story

6. Make It Relevant

Connect Bible stories to children's everyday lives. Help them see how David's courage applies when they face bullies at school, or how Joseph's forgiveness relates to conflicts with siblings. When kids see the Bible as relevant to their world, they pay attention.

7. Vary Your Teaching Methods

Don't rely on just one approach. Mix it up with:

  • Storytelling
  • Crafts and hands-on activities
  • Small group discussions
  • Games and competitions
  • Music and worship
  • Drama and role-playing

8. Create a Positive Environment

Children engage better when they feel safe and valued. Learn their names, celebrate their contributions, and create a classroom culture where every child feels welcome. Positive reinforcement goes much further than criticism.

9. Use Technology Wisely

Today's children are digital natives. Incorporate age-appropriate technology like:

  • Bible apps with interactive features
  • Educational videos
  • Digital games that reinforce lessons
  • Online quizzes and polls

Just remember—technology should enhance, not replace, personal interaction and hands-on learning.

10. End with Application

Always conclude with a clear, actionable takeaway. Give children one specific thing they can do during the week to apply what they've learned. When kids leave with a mission, they're more likely to remember and internalize the lesson.

Putting It All Together

You don't need to use all ten strategies in every lesson. Start by incorporating two or three new approaches and see what works best for your class. Every group of children is different, so be willing to adapt and experiment.

Remember, the goal isn't just to keep kids busy—it's to help them encounter God's Word in meaningful ways. When we engage their minds, hearts, and bodies, we create opportunities for the Holy Spirit to work in their lives.

What strategies have worked best in your Sunday School class? I'd love to hear your experiences and ideas in the comments below!
